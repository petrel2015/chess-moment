Two Rooks: Five Forcing Checks Down the Ladder

Not a one-move mate. Two rooks take turns sealing the ranks, squeezing the black king down five flights.

Difficulty 3/5　·　≈ 6 min　·　5 key decisions　·　Theme：Rook ladder and continuous forcing moves

Two rooks work like a pair of alternately closing doors: one gives check, the other guards the retreat, then they swap roles. Understanding this rhythm matters more than memorizing the final mating square.

You do not need to finish the game on move five. Your goal is to give check five times in a row, driving the black king from the eighth rank down to the fourth, while keeping the rooks protected in staggered layers.

Why the ladder mate suits beginnersEuropean kids' chess classes often draw the two-rook mate as a staircase. Each check pushes the king down one step, so students can see how control of space shrinks step by step — and how heavy pieces need to cooperate.

Today's Challenge

Give check five times in a row

Advance the rooks alternately. Every move must give check, and never place both rooks on a line where the king can easily attack them.

AI Settings

Review: One rook pushes, one rook seals

  1. Rb8+, Ra7+ first build the staggered seal pair.
  2. Rb6+, Ra5+ repeat the same structure instead of reinventing a plan every move.
  3. Rb4+ shows a combination may aim for a stable structure rather than an immediate mate.
  4. Before the mate lands, the white king still approaches to protect the front rook.
Takeaway: The value of continuous forcing moves is repeating a reliable structure: one piece makes the threat, another seals the escape.
