Whom You Trade First Decides a Whole Rook

Five straight choices. Don't rush to count what you capture now — add up the net gain of the whole exchange sequence.

Difficulty 4/5　·　≈ 8 min　·　5 key decisions　·　Theme：Exchange order and local material maximization

Beginners often grab whatever piece is capturable on sight; stronger players put every recapture on the same ledger. The first bite being the biggest does not mean you end with the most.

In this position White temporarily gives up a rook and a bishop, but every move carries a forcing threat. Your goal is not immediate mate but dismantling Black's defenses in the right order and cashing in the net gain on move five.

The initiative is the currency of exchangesNineteenth-century combinative players discovered early that a checking capture forces a reply, letting you stretch a seemingly losing exchange into a full combination. When calculating exchanges, count not only piece values but who holds the next move.

Today's Challenge

Five moves to cash in the whole local exchange

After each choice the opponent plays the most forcing reply. Don't just look at the immediate gain or loss — judge the net result after move five.

Review: Treat the five moves as one transaction

  1. Rxe8+ first removes the most important back-rank defender and lures the knight to recapture.
  2. Bxf7+ and Ng5+ keep using checks, leaving Black no pause to reorganize.
  3. Qb3+ forces the bishop to e6; Qxe6+ is the final payoff.
  4. Local material maximization comes from exchange order, the initiative and placement — not just piece prices.
Takeaway: When calculating exchanges, keep going until the forcing moves run out, then compare the final material; do not stop after the first capture.
